# Monday Morning Punchlines — candidates (manual ranking only)

Competition: u-gentle-badger-68-monday-morning-punchlines-709645dc ($5, pot escrowed).
Judge brief, in order: (1) really about Monday mornings; (2) real laugh, a punchline
that lands, not just a complaint; (3) surprise — twist is not the first thing anyone
would think of; (4) few words, every word working; (5) fresh, not a tired Monday cliche.
No Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge API/proxy tournament was used — ranking
below is manual, criterion by criterion, plus pairwise checks against the strongest
rival shapes in the field (alarm-clock lines, coffee lines, "I hate Mondays" complaints).

## Candidates

1. "I set five alarms for Monday. They formed a union and went on strike." (69 chars)
   Rationale: the alarms themselves rebel — the tool meant to beat Monday joins
   Monday's side. A real turn, not a complaint.
2. "Monday knocked. I pretended to be out. It let itself in." (56 chars)
   Rationale: Monday as an uninvited visitor who ignores the door; three short
   beats, punchline in the last four words.
3. "Monday is just Sunday with a tie and a grudge." (46 chars)
   Rationale: one image, Sunday dressed up and resentful; very economical.
4. "Monday morning is the weekend's free trial expiring." (52 chars)
   Rationale: the weekend as software with a trial period — a fresh frame.
5. "My bed and Monday are in a custody battle. Monday keeps winning mornings." (73 chars)
   Rationale: custody battle over the sleeper; Monday wins the mornings.
6. "My alarm didn't go off Monday. It filed for a transfer to Tuesday." (66 chars)
   Rationale: the alarm quits its job and requests a transfer — personification.
7. "I told Monday I'd call it back. It's been ringing since 6 a.m." (62 chars)
   Rationale: Monday as a persistent caller; the ringing never stops.
8. "Monday morning: my calendar's way of saying we need to talk." (60 chars)
   Rationale: the dreaded relationship talk, delivered by a calendar.
9. "I woke up Monday and my motivation had already called in sick." (62 chars)
   Rationale: motivation phones in sick before the day starts.
10. "Monday: the day my to-do list files a restraining order against me." (67 chars)
    Rationale: the to-do list legally distances itself from its owner.
11. "The weekend ended so fast even Monday looked surprised." (55 chars)
    Rationale: even Monday is caught off guard by how fast the weekend went.
12. "My coffee looked at Monday and asked for a coffee." (50 chars)
    Rationale: the coffee itself needs coffee — the classic, turned one notch.

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order)

- Criterion 1 (about Monday mornings): all 12 pass — every line names Monday or
  Monday morning directly. No stray-topic lines.
- Criterion 2 (real laugh, punchline lands): #1, #2, #5, #6, #10 have a genuine
  turn. #9 and #12 are complaints with a shrug, not a punchline; #11 has no turn
  at all — it just observes. #3 is a quip, a smile rather than a laugh.
- Criterion 3 (surprise): #1 wins — nobody's first thought is that the alarms
  unionise. #5 (custody battle) and #10 (restraining order) are second; #6
  (transfer to Tuesday) third. #9 (motivation called in sick) and #12 (coffee
  needs coffee) are the first thing anyone would think of — the field will be
  full of them.
- Criterion 4 (economy): #3 (46) and #12 (50) are tightest, but #1 wastes
  nothing either — "five" sets up the union, "Monday" anchors it, every word
  earns its place. #5 spends words on setup ("custody battle" needs the second
  sentence to pay off).
- Criterion 5 (freshness): #1, #4 (free trial), #5, #10 are fresh frames.
  #9 and #12 are reruns of the two tiredest Monday cliches (no motivation,
  need coffee). #11 is a generic "weekend too short" line.

## Pairwise vs the strongest rival shapes

- vs alarm-clock field ("my alarm went off, I didn't / I snoozed five times"):
  #1 beats it — same prop, but the alarms are the ones rebelling, so the joke
  is about the alarm, not about me being lazy. #6 is a weaker version of the
  same idea (one alarm transfers vs five alarms strike collectively) — #1 wins
  the pair on scale and on the word "union".
- vs coffee field ("coffee needs coffee / Monday needs coffee"): #12 IS that
  field, and loses to #1 on criteria 3 and 5 — it is the first thing anyone
  thinks of, and the field will be flooded with it.
- vs "I hate Mondays" complaint field: #9 and #11 sit in it and lose to #1 on
  criterion 2 — they list what's wrong with Mondays instead of landing a joke.
- vs #2 (Monday knocked): #2 is tighter (56 vs 69 chars) and its punchline is
  clean, but its turn — an unwanted visitor letting itself in — is a familiar
  image, while #1's union strike is a genuinely unexpected twist. #1 wins on
  criteria 2 and 3 by a clear margin; #2 is the runner-up.
- vs #5 (custody battle): #5's frame is fresh, but the punchline ("Monday
  keeps winning mornings") restates the setup rather than twisting it — #1's
  strike lands harder.

## Winner

**#1 — "I set five alarms for Monday. They formed a union and went on strike."**
69 characters. Wins on criteria 2 and 3 by the widest margin, passes 1, wastes
nothing on 4, and is fresh on 5. Runner-up: #2.
